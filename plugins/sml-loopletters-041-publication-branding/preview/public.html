The semis bid is thinner than it looks

Three weeks of gains on falling volume is not a rally, it is a drift. The distinction matters because the two unwind very differently, and right now the options market is pricing one while the tape shows the other.

What the skew is saying

January skew has been flattening since the second week of July. In a genuine risk-on move you would expect the opposite: calls bid, puts offered, the whole curve tilting. Instead the market is paying up for downside while spot grinds higher.

Flat skew into a rising tape is the market telling you it does not believe the tape.

That divergence has resolved toward the options market in four of the last five instances since 2019. Not a large sample, but the mechanism is sound — dealers hedge what they are short, and they are short downside.

The three names carrying it

Breadth inside the index has narrowed to a degree that is hard to overstate. Strip out the top three names and the sector is flat on the quarter.

NameContributionVolume vs 30d
Top 3+8.4%−22%
Next 12+0.3%−31%
Remainder−1.1%−28%

Falling volume across all three buckets is the part worth sitting with. Rallies that nobody participates in do not need a catalyst to end — they need only the marginal buyer to stop.

I am not short. I am, for the first time since April, no longer long.
